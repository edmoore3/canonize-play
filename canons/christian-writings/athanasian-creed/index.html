What it is

A Latin creed named after Athanasius but composed in the fifth century, probably in southern Gaul, setting out the Trinity and the Incarnation in balanced, repetitive clauses. It opens and closes by warning that holding this faith is necessary for salvation.

Why it matters

The most precise creedal statement of Western Trinitarian doctrine. It was used in Western worship and endorsed by the Lutheran, Anglican, and Reformed confessions, though its condemning clauses have long made it controversial.
